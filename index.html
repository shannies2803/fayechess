Where the progress goes

Every player's badges, stars and rating are saved by this browser, on this device only. There is no account and nothing is uploaded — there is no server to upload it to. Clearing your browser data will clear all of it, so use Back up below before you do.

What is in here

Three levels decide what a child ever sees. Brand new starts with how the pieces move and is never shown a long forced mate. I can play gets puzzles up to 1600 and the openings. I play matches gets everything, including the hardest ladder. Tap a name below to change it.

The chess itself

Every position was checked by a chess engine: the mates are proved forced against every defence, and each star puzzle was solved by computer to confirm it is possible within the star target. In the beginner lessons the shortest route to every star was found by exhaustive search, so the gold target is the true best and not a guess. The opponent is a small built-in engine; its strength is set by the three buttons under “Just play a game”.

Nothing to worry about

No ads, no chat, no links out, no timers, no lives to lose. A wrong move is always just "try again".

Harder puzzles, properly rated

The built-in puzzles are graded by a measure I wrote. Lichess rates theirs by how millions of real players actually performed, which is better. Take a slice of their free database and drop it in — every puzzle is checked by the engine before it is accepted, and the imported set is shared by everyone using this device.

curl -s https://database.lichess.org/lichess_db_puzzle.csv.zst | zstd -dc | awk -F, '$4>1200 && $4<1600' | head -600 > faye.csv

Who is playing

Each child keeps their own stars, rating and daily four, and only sees content at their own level. Switch between them with the row of names at the top of the home screen.

Getting ready for a competition

What this app can do on its own, and what it needs you for.

Every day, ten minutes. The four things on the home screen. The 🔁 items are the ones she got wrong before — those are the ones that make the difference, so do not let her skip them.

Once a week, one slow game. "Just play a game" on Toughest, with the clock on. That opponent finds the right move in about nine positions out of ten up to a rating of 1900, so it will not hand her anything. Losing is the point.

After every real game she plays, go through it. "Look at a game again" → tap the moves out → it will show you the moment it turned. This is the hour that a coach is mostly paid for, and you can do it. The one question the app cannot ask is the useful one: what were you thinking when you played that? Ask that, and listen.

Before the tournament: she should be able to write moves down — "Write the game down" drills it — because a scoresheet is what lets her bring the game home. Check she still knows her three openings; the openings list marks the ones due for a look with 🔁.

On the day: eat first, and go to the toilet between rounds rather than during. Sit on her hands if she moves too fast. After a loss the rule is: shake hands, walk outside, and do not look at the game until the tournament is over — there is another round to play and nothing good comes of chewing on it now. Go through it that evening.

What this cannot give her is somebody noticing the habit she does not know she has, and the experience of playing people rather than a program. If you do take lessons, ask the coach to spend most of the time on her own games rather than lecturing — and a club, where she plays other children over a board, is usually worth more per hour than a private coach at her age.
